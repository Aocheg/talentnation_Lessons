## 2. SEARCH ENGINES.
- Search Engines handles retrieval tools. you type "best phone under 100k naira" and it returns links with the request typed. However behind the scenes, search engines use many AI techniques. 
## Search Engines Handles: 
    - Spelling errors and variations: If you type “restarant in Lekki” instead of “restaurant in Lekki,” the engine still knows what you mean.
    - Synonyms and related concepts: If you search “how to fix generator,” it may return results about “troubleshooting generator problems” even if the exact words differ.
    - Query intent: If you search “Bolt vs Uber Lagos,” the engine infers you want a comparison, not just the homepages of both companies.
    - Ranking: From millions of matching pages, it must decide the most useful order.
    - Freshness and context: A search for “current fuel price in Nigeria” needs recent information, not old pages.

## WHy Does this Qualify as AI?
- Search engines must handle language understanding, pattern recognition, and reasoning under uncertainty. They learn from billions of queries, clicks, and user behaviors to improve. A fixed rule like “return pages containing the exact words” would produce terrible results because language is messy and varied.

For example, if someone types “plumber near me,” the engine must understand that “near me” depends on the user’s location, that “plumber” means a person who fixes pipes, and that the user probably wants contact details and reviews. This requires more than simple keyword matching.

Search engines are AI because they continuously learn and adapt to language and user needs.

## FRAUS DETECTION.
- Banks and payment companies process millions of transactions every day. Some of those transactions are legitimate. Some are fraudulent. The challenge is telling the difference quickly and accurately.

In Nigeria, with the growth of POS transactions, mobile transfers, and online payments, fraud detection is serious business. A customer may receive a text message from their bank saying a transaction was flagged. The bank may have blocked a suspicious transfer.

How does the bank know? Fraud detection systems use AI to learn patterns of normal and abnormal behavior.

## A fraud detection system might look at:
    - The amount of the transaction.
    - The location of the user.
    - The type of device used.
    - The time of day.
    - The user’s transaction history.
    - Whether the transaction is unusual compared with past behavior.

## WHy does this Qualify as AI?
- Fraud patterns change. Fraudsters constantly develop new tricks. A rule like “block transactions over ₦500,000” would miss smaller fraud and annoy customers with large legitimate purchases. AI can learn from new fraud cases and adapt.

- Fraud detection involves uncertainty. The system rarely knows for certain that a transaction is fraudulent. It estimates risk and decides whether to approve, flag, or decline.

- It handles complex patterns across many variables. Human analysts cannot manually review every transaction, but an AI system can evaluate thousands per second.

- Fraud detection is a clear example of AI because it requires learning from data, recognizing patterns, and making decisions under uncertainty.

## GENRATIVE AI.
- Generative AI is a more recent and highly visible form of AI. It creates new content: text, images, audio, code, and more.

- You may have used ChatGPT, Google Gemini, or Meta AI to write an email, generate a business idea, or explain a concept. Maybe you have seen apps that create images from text descriptions, or tools that compose music.

- Generative AI systems learn patterns from large amounts of existing content. They then generate new content that resembles what they have learned. For example, a language model learns how words and sentences typically flow together. When you give it a prompt like “Write a polite email requesting a refund,” it generates a new email that follows learned patterns.

## Why does this qualify as AI?

- Generative AI requires learning on a massive scale. It also requires pattern recognition and reasoning to produce coherent, relevant output. It handles variability: the same prompt can produce different responses depending on context and the model’s training.

- Generative AI is often called creative, but it is really pattern-based generation. It does not understand the content the way a human does. It predicts what is likely to come next based on learned patterns. This is why it can sometimes produce fluent but incorrect information. Still, its ability to generate new text, images, and ideas is associated with intelligence.

## Why each example qualifies as AI.
In each case, the system:

- Learns from data rather than following only fixed rules.
- Handles variable inputs and incomplete information.
- Makes decisions under uncertainty.
- Improves as it receives more data.

--**That is the heart of AI**.
