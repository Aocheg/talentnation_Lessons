# Tier 5 — Architecture Challenge: Explain the Whole System

## 1. Introduction

An online store is a system that receives information from customers, follows programmed instructions to process that information, stores order details, and communicates results to customers.

In this example, a customer selects a product that costs **₦50,000** and orders **2 units**.

## 2. Customer input

The customer provides information to the online store by selecting a product and entering the quantity they want to buy.

- **Product:** The item selected by the customer.
- **Quantity:** 2 units, entered by the customer.
- **Product price:** ₦50,000 per unit, supplied by the store's product information.

The price is available from the store's product data; it is not necessarily entered by the customer.

## 3. Program and instructions

The **program** is the online store application. It contains instructions that tell the computer how to handle the customer's selection and order.

Instructions act as a guide by defining the actions the program should perform and the order in which they should happen. For example, the program can retrieve the product price, read the quantity, calculate the total, and save the order details.

## 4. Processing

The system calculates the order total by multiplying the price per unit by the quantity ordered.

**Calculation:**

₦50,000 × 2 = **₦100,000**

- **Input:** Product price of ₦50,000 and quantity of 2.
- **Processing:** Multiplication.
- **Output of calculation:** ₦100,000.

## 5. Stored data

After an order is placed, the online store needs to store relevant information about it. This may include:

- Product name or identifier.
- Product price at the time of the order.
- Quantity ordered.
- Total order price.
- Customer or delivery information needed for the order.
- Order date and time.
- Order status, such as pending, processed, shipped, or delivered.

The store saves this information for record keeping, accountability, order tracking, and customer support. If a customer asks about an order or reports a problem, the store can retrieve the saved record.

## 6. Output

After processing the order, the store can display the total price and an order confirmation to the customer.

For this example, the displayed total is **₦100,000**. The confirmation can also show a summary of the product and quantity and indicate that the order was received.

## 7. The complete system flow

1. **Real-world request:** A customer wants to buy a product.
2. **Input:** The customer selects the product and enters a quantity of 2.
3. **Data:** The system uses the selected product's information, including its price of ₦50,000.
4. **Program and instructions:** The online store follows its programmed rules to handle the order.
5. **Processing:** The system multiplies ₦50,000 by 2.
6. **Stored data:** The system saves the relevant order details so they can be retrieved later.
7. **Output:** The customer sees the total of ₦100,000 and an order confirmation.

## 8. Key concepts

### What is a computer?

A computer is an electronic machine that receives data, processes it according to instructions, stores information, and produces or communicates output to users.

### What is a program?

A program is a set of instructions written for a computer to follow. Instructions guide the computer by telling it what actions to perform and in what order.

### What is data?

Data is information represented in a form that a computer can store, process, and communicate. An online store needs data for record keeping, accountability, order tracking, and customer support.

### What information does the online store need to store?

The store needs relevant product, customer, and order information. For this example, that includes the product identifier or name, price, quantity, total, necessary customer or delivery details, order date, and order status. The exact information stored depends on what is needed to fulfil and manage the order.

## 9. Final reflection

A computer does not understand the real world directly. It works with representations of information and follows instructions for processing those representations.

In the online store example, the customer's request is represented as data. The program follows instructions to calculate the total, stores the order details, and produces an output that the customer can understand. This shows how a real-world problem can be handled through data, a program, instructions, processing, stored data, and output.
